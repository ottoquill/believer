## Session Fourteen

*Transcript — couples therapy, session 14. Audio, partially indexed. Names redacted to initials per release. Practitioner: Dr. M—, LMFT.*

**DR. M—:** Before we start, I want to acknowledge what's in the room with us. You both signed the new intake addendum. You understand that what's said here today is — let's use the word the lawyers put on the form —

**R—:** Permanent.

**DR. M—:** I was going to say *irreversible.* But yes. We can't take things back the way we used to. So we go slow. K—, last time you wanted to talk about feeling unseen. Can you say more?

**K—:** I feel — [pause] — I started to say *unseen* but that isn't true. He sees me. That's the problem.

**R—:** [laughs] There it is.

**DR. M—:** Let's stay with K—. K—, when you say he sees you and that's the problem, what's the feeling under it?

**K—:** I want him to *pretend* sometimes. That's all. When I come home and I've had the day I've had, I want him to tell me I look nice. And he won't.

**R—:** I can't.

**K—:** I *know.*

**R—:** I tried last Tuesday. I got to "you look" and then — it was like a camera went off behind my eyes, this little white flash, and that taste, foil on the back of the tongue. Gone in a second. But I lost the sentence.

**DR. M—:** And that was a small one. For the record. That was about as small as they come.

**R—:** It was the size of a kindness. The smallest possible kindness, and it still went off in my face.

**DR. M—:** K—, can you hear what he's saying? He isn't withholding. He's *unable.*

**K—:** I hear it. Every day. He's *unable.* It's very convenient, being unable. He gets to be honest, I get to be hideous, and whoever's doing this to the building gets to keep us all so beautifully *real.*

**DR. M—:** We don't actually know that it's a person. The mechanism. There are theories —

**R—:** It's a person. The man in 9B travels every other week, and those are the only weeks I can tell K— her cooking's good.

**K—:** Is it good?

**R—:** [silence, eleven seconds]

**DR. M—:** R—, you don't have to answer that. Silence is —

**R—:** Silence is its own answer in here. You taught us that. Session four.

**DR. M—:** I did. I taught you a great many things in here that worked beautifully when I could send you home and let you fill in the gaps. The whole field worked on the gaps. We built it on reframing. On helping people find the *generous interpretation.* "He didn't mean it that way." "She's doing her best." [pause] I had a script. Twenty years of script.

**K—:** What happened to the script?

**DR. M—:** A man moved into the building.

**R—:** [laughs]

**DR. M—:** Let's come back to the cooking. K—, you asked. He went quiet. What did the quiet tell you?

**K—:** That it's not good. That it was never good. That for eleven years he ate it and said *delicious,* and I built a whole — I thought I was good at *one thing,* in the whole marriage. I thought, all right, I'm difficult, I'm a lot, but the man eats like a king. And he sat there. Eleven years. With his fork. *Lying.*

**R—:** They weren't lies. They were —

**K—:** Don't.

**R—:** They were *the marriage.* That's what the marriage *was,* most of it. A thousand small —

**DR. M—:** Let's name it carefully, because this is the thing I see in every room now, every couple, since the building. R—, what you're describing — the small daily generosities that aren't strictly accurate — there used to be a soft word for those, and I notice you won't say it.

**R—:** [pause] If I say the word, will it hurt?

**DR. M—:** Saying the *word* won't hurt. The word's true. It only hurts to *tell* one. We can name the species without committing the act. That's most of what therapy is now. Naming the species.

**R—:** Then. The marriage was mostly those. The kind ones, the ones nobody counts. "I'm not tired." "I don't mind." "It's fine, go, have fun with them." I told her a hundred of those a week, and I meant the *love* under them even when I didn't mean the *words,* and now I can't say a single one. What's left is — [pause] — just the facts. We're two people who've been handed nothing but the facts about each other, and it turns out the facts were never the marriage. The facts were the furniture. The kind lies were the house.

**DR. M—:** [pause] That's — I'm going to write that down.

**K—:** [crying] Say something true that's also kind. Both at once. You used to be able to do both at once.

**R—:** [long silence]

**DR. M—:** Take your time, R—. The sentence exists. It's just narrow.

**R—:** [pause] I'm still here.

**K—:** That's it?

**R—:** That's the one that doesn't burn. I checked it before I said it. I — God, listen to me. I *audit* my sentences now. Before I open my mouth to my own wife I run them past the — I check for the flash. "I'm still here." True. Safe. So I'm allowed it. "I love you the way I did at the start —" [winces] — that one, that one stings, K—, I'm sorry, it just — because it isn't the same as the start, nothing's the same as the start, and it doesn't care that I meant it tender. It only cares that it isn't *accurate.*

**K—:** Stop. Stop checking. I don't want the ones that pass. I want the ones that would have hurt you. Those were the real ones. Those were the gifts.

**DR. M—:** [pause] I want to gently note the time. And I want to be honest, since I can't be anything else in here either. I drove past the building this morning, before work. I parked across from it. I have a sister I haven't spoken to in nine years and a thing I've never said to her, and I thought: if I went up there, if I stood in the right apartment, I'd *have* to. It would simply come out. The man's a confessional you can't walk out of. And I sat in the car and couldn't decide whether that was the worst thing I'd ever imagined or the only honest hour I'd ever get. And then I drove here. To do this. Whatever this is now.

**K—:** What is it now? What are we doing?

**DR. M—:** [pause] I used to think I helped people tell each other the truth. Turns out I helped them *survive* not having to. That was the whole job. I worked for the kindness, not the meaning. And the kindness has gone — not from me, from the *air* — and I don't know what I'm for anymore. I'm a swimming instructor in a city that drained the pools.

**R—:** [quietly] You're still here.

**DR. M—:** [pause] — that's mine, isn't it. You're handing me my own line.

**R—:** It's the only one that doesn't draw blood. I'm spreading it around.

**K—:** [laughs, then doesn't]

**DR. M—:** Let's stop there. Same time next week. And — K—, R— — I'm not going to tell you it gets easier. I checked the sentence. It doesn't pass.

*[End of session. Note appended in Dr. M—'s hand: "Refer out? To whom. Everyone's two floors down from someone now."]*
