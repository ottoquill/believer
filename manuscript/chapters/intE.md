## The Sermon at the Fairgrounds

*Transcribed from a recording posted to the Conviction's channel under the title FAIRGROUNDS — NIGHT ONE. Approx. four thousand in attendance. Bracketed notes by the transcriptionist, a volunteer. Crowd sounds left in at the prophet's request: "Leave the breathing. People should hear that a room can stop hurting."*

—testing. There. Can the back hear me. Don't answer. You'll feel it if I lie to you, and I won't, so there's nothing to test.

[laughter]

I'm not going to shout tonight. A man shouts when he's not sure you'll believe him. I'm sure. Sit on the grass if your feet are tired. God built the grass before He built the folding chair, and I trust the older work.

Some of you drove a long way. I want you to forget the city tonight. The city is where they keep the man. You've seen him — grey suit, grey face, never smiles. They put him on your television and call him a curse that fell on the world, and ever since, the world has been wringing its hands and asking *why, why has this terrible thing come.*

I stood next to that man. In a field, an hour from here, in the morning light. They told me I'd fall down. They told me a man can't stand inside his shadow and speak and not bleed for it.

[pause]

I did not bleed.

I said the truest words I know — that there is a God, that He made you, that He loves you — and I said them eight feet from the one man alive who would know if I were lying, and look. Look at me. No blood. No wince. Nothing. I felt the great clean nothing of a man with no lie in him to find.

And do you know what he did, the man they call the Believer? I'll tell you, because the cameras caught it and they'll show you everything but this. I held out my hand and I asked him, plain, in front of four hundred souls: *Tell them I'm a liar. You're the one man on earth who'd know. Say it.*

He opened his mouth.

And nothing came out.

[a sound from the crowd]

Nothing came out. Because he could not. Because the gift they sold you as a curse is a scale, friends, and it does not weigh the man it weighs the doubt in him, and when he weighed me he found me clean, and his own silence said so louder than any sermon I could preach. They built that man to burn liars. I stood in his fire and was not burned. What does that make me? What does that make any of you who walk in unafraid?

It makes you the faithful. The fire burns the liars and blesses the faithful, and it cannot tell a curse from a blessing, because the difference was never in the fire. It was always in you.

[She is crying, the woman in the front. There are several now.]

Don't be ashamed of it. You've been carrying a weight a year now and longer. I know what it is. You go to your mother's bedside and she asks are the tests good, and you cannot say *they're good, Mama, rest now* — you used to give her that small mercy, and now it lances you, the lie punished before it clears your teeth. You go to your boy with his bad report card and his big wet eyes and you cannot tell him it'll be all right. The whole tender architecture of how we used to love each other — all the soft untruths we laid down like straw so the hard floor wouldn't bruise the people we love — gone. Overnight. And you've been asking, every one of you, in the dark: *what kind of God does this to His children.*

I'll tell you what kind.

The kind who loves you too much to watch you lie yourselves to death one more year.

Because that's what we were doing. Be honest — you're near enough to me now that you'll have to be. We were dying of it. Lie to the wife, lie to the mirror, lie to the boy until the boy grows up fluent in it and lies to his own. We called it kindness. It was rot, and it was spreading, and the only physician with the nerve to cut was God.

[pause]

So why does it hurt?

The doctors won't say. The man in the grey suit can't say — he only suffers it, poor soul, same as you, the most afflicted man in the country and too proud to be cured. But I'll say it. Lean in. I'll say it the way I say everything, where it would cost me if it weren't so:

Pain is doubt leaving the body.

[a long murmur]

Every time it hurts to speak — every time the kind word turns to a hot wire on your tongue — that is not punishment, beloved. That is surgery. That is the doubt in you, the hidden disbelief, the little cold splinter of *maybe it isn't so,* coming up out of you the only road it has, through the nerve. It hurts because you are still half a doubter. It hurts because some sealed room in you still won't *believe.*

And the cure was never to lie better. There's a whole city of men who lie for a living and not one of them found peace in it; they only got rich. The cure is to stop needing to. To close the gap. To believe a thing so far down that there is no daylight left between you and it for the pain to crawl through. Believe your mother is held in the hands of God, and it will not cost you to say so at her bedside, because it will be true in you, and the scale will weigh you and find you clean.

That is the whole of it. That is the only medicine. Not distance. Not a soundproofed room you rent by the hour from men who'd sell you back your own silence. *Belief.* Free, and yours, and waiting.

You came here tonight in pain. I'm telling you that you do not have to leave in it.

[He has them quiet now. The recording catches wind in the field PA.]

There's a man across the river who proved, with his own ruined silence, that I am telling you the truth. He won't say it out loud. He can't bring himself to. But you felt it land just now, didn't you — no flinch, no blood, only a field full of people hearing a thing that's so. That is the only proof there is, and the only proof you'll ever need.

Now stand up off the grass. All of you. Take the hand of the stranger next to you, who is not a stranger, who is your brother in the Doubt and your brother out of it.

And say it back to me, so I can hear the whole field say it at once:

Believe—

[crowd: *Believe—*]

—and you will not hurt.

[The recording runs another nine minutes. It is only the one sentence, over and over, four thousand voices, and toward the end you cannot hear a single one of them wince.]
