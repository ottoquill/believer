## Set List

*Transcript — late set, the Basement Room (downtown), recorded for an unreleased special. Eleven months post-onset. [Audience response in brackets.]*

— okay, settle, settle. You paid for the second drink, not for me. Thank you for being here, in a *room*, with a *body*, which is the only place anything still counts.

[laughter]

So my agent says, do a clean set, the Conviction people are litigious. I said Marty, relax. I'm an artist. Art doesn't hurt.

[laughter]

No, listen — that's the law. Look it up. A guy onstage saying a thing that isn't so, that's *fiction*, that's *protected*, no charge. I can stand up here and tell you my marriage is wonderful —

[laughter]

— see? Smooth as glass. Try that at home. My wife asks how she looks, I open my mouth, I taste a nine-volt battery. Up here I'm a free man. Last free country in America, two-drink minimum.

[applause]

Guy comes up after the early show. Says, you ever wonder why the stage gets a pass? I told him, every night around the third joke. It doesn't hurt because nobody up here means a word of it. We get the exemption for being *insincere*. They handed the liars a license and called it the arts.

[laughter, scattered]

That's the deal. An actor says "I love you" eight shows a week and bleeds for none of it. A priest says it once and means it and the wall comes down. Means it. Imagine. Meaning a thing. Couldn't be me.

[laughter]

I do a bit about my father. He's dead — that part's true, the rest I built because the true part wasn't funny. And *that's* allowed. That's the whole racket. I take a real man and I *reframe* him till you laugh, and the field never touches me, because we both know it's a bit. You know it's a bit. Right? You —

VOICE (from the dark, near the bar): Do you, though.

— folks, we have a participant.

[laughter]

VOICE: I said do you, though. Know it's a bit.

— sir, the heckler portion is later, when you're drunker and I'm funnier —

VOICE: You said the stage gets a pass because nobody up here means it. So mean something. One true thing. Let's see if your hands shake.

[uneasy laughter]

— see, this is the new crowd. Everybody's a doubter or a believer and nobody's just *out for a beer.* Fine. You want to play. I'll run the bit about my dad, you tell me when the lying starts. Ready. My father was a good man —

[pause]

— huh. Give me a second.

VOICE: Take your time.

— that's the joke, the joke is he wasn't. Normally this kills, because we all agree there's a *character* up here, a guy named Dad, and the line's just air. But you've got me *meaning* it now. And the line's still false. So it's not a bit anymore. It's a man saying a kind lie about his dead father and meaning it, and —

[silence]

— there. You see that? Front row saw it. Little wince. Ladies and gentlemen, I appear to have *meant* something false, and the room billed me on the spot.

[no laughter]

VOICE: Pain is doubt leaving the body.

— don't. Don't bring their thing into my room.

VOICE: I'm only saying what we watched. The joke cost you nothing. The second you meant it, you paid. So which part were you lying about — the bit, or the laugh you wanted us to hand you for it?

[murmuring]

— hell of a question for a basement. The exemption's not a *loophole*, friend, it's the deal. You come in, you know it's pretend, and I get to be brave with the things I'm too scared to mean. That's not a lie. That's a kindness with the lights down.

VOICE: Then why's it the one room left where a man says anything and walks out clean? Doesn't it bother you — that the comedians and the cult leaders are the only two kinds of people who never bleed?

[long silence]

— okay. You got the room. Take it. I'll give you a true one. No character, no dad, just me, and you watch my hands.

I'm scared every night that the only reason this still works is that we haven't decided yet whether a joke is a man telling the truth sideways or a man getting away with something. And the night you out there start asking, *if it didn't hurt him, did he even mean it* — that's the night the last room closes. Then it's just the ones who can't lie and the ones who taught themselves to believe everything they say. Nobody in the middle. Nobody laughing.

[silence]

— how were my hands.

VOICE: Steady.

— yeah. That's the part that scares me.

[a few people leave. No applause.]

— that's my time. You've been *here*, which is the most anyone can be. Tip your waitstaff. Believe whatever gets you to your car.
