## NTSB Factual Report - Excerpt

*National Transportation Safety Board — Aircraft Accident Factual Report — Supplemental Findings (Reopened) — Public Docket*

**1. FACTUAL INFORMATION**

**1.1 History of Flight**

The aircraft, a regional jet operated under Part 121, departed on a scheduled domestic flight with 4 crew and 43 passengers aboard. Approximately 19 minutes after takeoff, in the climb, the flight crew reported "an asymmetry" and "a problem with the controls." The cockpit voice recording is consistent with a sudden uncommanded movement of a flight control surface. Control was not regained. There were no survivors among the 47 occupants. The original report identified eleven of the dead by category as fatalities attributable to "the event sequence." The present supplement does not revise the occupant total. It corrects the cause.

**1.2 Component Examination**

Recovered components of the actuator assembly (P/N withheld pending litigation) were examined at the Materials Laboratory. The fracture surface exhibited features consistent with fatigue initiating at a subsurface inclusion, propagating under cyclic loading well below the qualification threshold of record. In plain terms: the part failed long before the paperwork said it could.

The original report attributed the loss to "an anomalous and unforeseeable single-point failure" and to crew response thereto. The reopening was prompted by documents produced in a civil proceeding and not available to the original investigators.

**1.3 Qualification Testing (Revised Findings)**

Examination of the manufacturer's qualification records, **Halcombe Aerospace**, revealed the following. The endurance test of record — which the component must survive to be certified — reported completion of the full required cycle count without failure. The test article had been removed from the rig prior to completion. The reported figures for the final cycles were not measured. They were entered.

A part that would have failed the test was certified by the test it failed. The Board notes only that the difference between a number measured and a number entered is invisible on the page and total in the air.

**1.4 Notification and Disposition**

An internal report flagging the discrepancy was raised by an engineer of the manufacturer approximately fourteen months prior to the accident. The report was reviewed. It was characterized, in subsequent communications, as the product of "an unreliable source" and "a disgruntled former employee with a documented pattern of unsubstantiated grievance." The engineer's professional credibility was, in the words of one recovered memorandum, "managed." The report was not forwarded to the certificating authority. No corrective action was initiated. The component remained in service.

The Board makes no finding as to motive. The Board finds only that the information existed, that it was correct, that it was available, and that it was characterized in such a way that it did not have to be believed.

**2. ANALYSIS**

The probable cause of this accident was the in-flight failure of an actuator component certified on the basis of a falsified endurance test, the discrepancy in which had been identified, documented, and suppressed prior to the accident. Contributing to the accident was a sustained course of conduct by which truthful information was made to appear unreliable.

No statute compels the disclosure of a fact one has persuaded oneself is not a fact. The mechanism by which an organization, and the individuals within it, came to regard a true report as false is outside the Board's jurisdiction. We investigate the wreckage. We do not investigate the certainty that made it.

**3. RECOMMENDATIONS**

To the certificating authority: that endurance-test data be witnessed and independently recorded, the entered number being indistinguishable, after the fact, from the honest one, and lethal in exactly the cases where it differs.

To the operator and manufacturer: no recommendation. The aircraft type is retired. The manufacturer no longer exists under the name above.

To the public: this report is entered into the docket in full. It will be read by approximately the number of persons who read such reports, which is to say almost none.

**APPENDIX C — DECEDENTS**

*(The Board includes the following at the request of next of kin. The names are listed not because the report requires them but because reports of this kind have historically omitted them, the total being easier to carry than the eleven.)*

1. A schoolteacher, 38, traveling home.
2. A retired machinist, 71.
3. An infant, 9 months, in arms.
4. The infant's mother, 29.
5. A college sophomore returning for a funeral.
6. A pediatric nurse, 44, off-shift.
7. A man who had, that week, been declared cancer-free.
8. A newlywed.
9. The newlywed's husband.
10. A salesman, 53, who flew this route weekly and was known by name to the crew.
11. A girl, 9, flying alone for the first time, whose tag, recovered from the site, listed under PERSON TO CONTACT a single name and a phone number — the line at which now rings, when dialed, and is not answered.

*The remaining 36 occupants are listed in the full docket. The eleven above were identified, in the original report, by category. They are named here because, at the request of the families, the Board wished the record to show that it had stopped pretending the number was the truth.*
