# A Note for Book Clubs {.unnumbered}

*Discussion questions for* Believer.

1. The novel never explains where Daniel's condition comes from. Did you want an explanation? What would the book have lost — or gained — if it gave you one?

2. Daniel begins as a man who lies for a living, and we are meant, at first, to admire how *good* he is at it. Did the opening seduce you? When did your sympathy shift — and did it ever fully return?

3. The condition punishes deceit but not sincere error: believe your own lie and it costs you nothing. Who in your own life would be safest standing in Daniel's presence — and what does that say about the relationship between honesty and certainty?

4. Nadia argues that mercy is the part of the truth people actually pay her for. Is a kind lie ever the more honest thing to say? Where is the line between mercy and cowardice?

5. Elias Crane feels no pain because he believes every word, including the false ones. Is he a villain? What does he offer people that the truth cannot — and why is it so persuasive?

6. The book insists that truth and goodness are not the same thing. Find a moment where a true sentence does more harm than any lie. Did the condition make Daniel a better person, or only a more accurate one?

7. June learns the loophole as a child, teaching herself to believe her own comforts. Was that a tragedy, a survival skill, or both?

8. Truth in the novel lives only where bodies are — phones, broadcasts, and writing carry lies freely. How did that distinction land for you, living as we do through screens?

9. The climax is a confession, not a fight, and the one truth that matters is the one the curse *cannot* force out of Daniel. Why does it matter that no one could make him say it?

10. The ending refuses to rule on whether a world of compelled truth is better or worse. Where did you come down — and did the person sitting next to you in your book club agree?
