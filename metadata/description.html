He cannot tell a lie. Neither can anyone in the room.

Daniel Mercer is the most gifted crisis-fixer of his generation—the man powerful people call at two in the morning to make a scandal disappear. He doesn't lie crudely. He reframes. He is very, very good at it.

Then one ordinary morning he discovers he can no longer speak a lie without pain—a pain that scales with the size of the lie, from a sting to a seizure. Worse: no one near him can lie either. Truth has acquired a radius, and Daniel is its unwilling center.

At first it looks like a gift. Witnesses recant. The wrongly convicted walk free. A dying father finally says the true thing to his son. But truth is not the same as mercy. In Daniel's presence the cruelest words are the truest ones, the small kind lies that hold a marriage together are suddenly impossible, and the people he loves can no longer be spared anything.

And there is a loophole. The pain spares anyone who truly believes what they say. The honest suffer; the certain go free. So when a preacher named Elias Crane stands inside Daniel's field and feels nothing—because he believes every word, including the false ones—he turns a curse meant to enforce truth into a creed: Believe, and you will never hurt again. A frightened nation learns that the cure for a world of unbearable truth is to simply stop knowing what's true.

To stop the prophet his own power created, Daniel will have to do the one thing no curse can force out of him: tell the truth about himself.

Believer is a literary novel built on the bones of a superhero origin and sharpened to the edge of satire—a propulsive, humane, and deeply unsettling story about honesty and cruelty, faith and self-deception, and the small mercies that quietly hold a life together. It asks the question we are all, suddenly, living inside: in a world drowning in lies, would the truth actually save us—or would we simply learn to believe whatever stopped the pain?

For readers of José Saramago's Blindness, Naomi Alderman's The Power, and Emily St. John Mandel's Station Eleven.
