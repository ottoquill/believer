## One-paragraph premise
Daniel Mercer, the most gifted crisis-communications fixer of his generation, wakes one morning unable to tell a lie without pain proportional to the lie — and discovers that no one in his physical presence can either. The condition is never explained. It punishes deceit but not sincere error, so the only refuge is to genuinely believe your own falsehoods; the honest suffer and the self-deceived go free. As the world learns what Daniel is, truth becomes a weapon, a sacrament, an instrument of torture, and a creed — and the man who built his life on lies must learn the one thing his power cannot give him: how to be good, not merely accurate.

## Setting & register
- **When:** Contemporary, present-day, unnamed-but-clearly-American media republic. Smartphones, cable news, podcasts, livestreams.
- **Where:** A large coastal U.S. city (unnamed; rendered as "the city") and Washington-adjacent power; later a federal black site and a heartland tent revival.
- **Register:** Literary realism for Daniel's chapters; biting satire for the dossier interludes; the architecture and escalation of a superhero origin, inverted (the "power" is a curse, the "villain" cannot be punched, the climax is a confession).
