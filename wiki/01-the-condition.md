# 01 — The Condition (canonical physics)

> The condition is **never explained** in the text. No scientist solves it, no angel confirms it, no origin myth is validated. Characters *theorize*; the book never adjudicates. Treat it as immutable law, rendered with the rigor of hard SF and the silence of scripture.

In-world, it has many names (see [07-canon-and-glossary.md](07-canon-and-glossary.md)): *the affliction, the condition, the gift, the field, the Mercer effect, the conviction.* The press settles on calling Daniel **"the Believer."**

---

## Rule 1 — The Compulsion
A **spoken lie** causes the speaker **physical pain**, arriving in the same instant as the words.

- The pain is **proportional to the lie's magnitude and consequence** — not to word count. A lie measured by how much reality it bends and how much harm it does.
- **Scale of pain (canonical calibration):**
  - *Trivial* ("I'm fine," "love the gift") → a sting, a wince: like biting foil, a paper cut on the soft palate, a flashbulb behind the eyes.
  - *Consequential* (a covered affair, a falsified number, a betrayed friend) → migraine-grade; nosebleed; the taste of a struck battery; knees give.
  - *Structural* (a lie that moves markets, sends an innocent to prison, starts a war) → maiming; seizure; burst vessels; **a large enough lie can kill the speaker.** Nobody has tested the ceiling on purpose and lived to confirm it.
- The pain **compounds** under sustained or repeated lying. A long con told in one sitting becomes unsurvivable before it finishes.
- The pain is **private and invisible** to others except by its effects (the wince, the sweat, the blood). This matters: observers infer truth from suffering, which is its own cruelty.

## Rule 2 — The Field (proximity)
**Anyone within Daniel's physical presence is under Rule 1.**

- **Range:** line-of-sight / earshot — practically, **a room (~10 meters / 30 feet).** Soft edge, not a laser line; right at the boundary lies cause only a vague unease (people instinctively drift to the edge of the room around him without knowing why).
- **Blocked by:** solid walls, distance, and being out of earshot. The field is *physical and local.*
- **NOT carried by:** telephones, radio, video calls, recordings, writing, or proxies. **A lie spoken into a phone does not hurt — even if Daniel hears it on the other end.** A lie written down never hurts.
- **Consequence (the book's spine):** *Truth now exists only where bodies are.* Every mediated channel — broadcast, print, the feed — remains a free country of lies. Presence becomes the only confessional. Power learns to keep its distance.
- Daniel **cannot turn it off, dial it down, or aim it.** It is always on, always the same radius. He is a walking jurisdiction he never asked to govern.
- The field affects **everyone** in range including Daniel, including infants and the demented (who simply cry or rage when they try to assert falsehoods), including people who don't know what he is.

## Rule 3 — The Belief Loophole (the dark engine of the plot)
A lie hurts **only if the speaker knows, in the moment, that it is false.** The condition punishes **deceit, not error.**

- **Sincere false belief is painless.** Say something untrue that you genuinely believe → no pain. The earth is flat, said by a true flat-earther → nothing.
- **Therefore the only escape is self-persuasion.** People near Daniel — and later, people who fear a world of Daniels — train themselves to *actually believe* what they wish were true. The deluded, the fanatical, and the devout pass through untouched.
- **The honest suffer; the self-deceived go free.** A scrupulous person who says "I might be wrong, but I think—" is safer than a confident liar but more tortured than a serene fanatic. **Doubt is painful; certainty is comfortable.** This is the moral inversion at the novel's heart.
- This loophole is **discovered partway through Part II** and lands as horror, not relief.
- **Antagonist corollary:** A person of perfect sincerity is *immune.* Elias Crane can stand in the field and feel nothing because he believes every word he says. The lie-detector has no power over the true believer.

## Rule 4 — Edge cases (rigorous; arbitrate consistently)
- **Silence / omission is allowed.** Refusing to answer causes no pain. But silence in the field is *legible* — its placement betrays as much as speech. "No comment" becomes a confession.
- **Questions, hypotheticals, and conditionals** are not assertions → safe. ("What if I said I didn't?" doesn't hurt.)
- **Sarcasm / irony understood as such** by speaker and audience → safe (no sincere assertion of falsehood). Misread sarcasm taken as a sincere claim → it can sting the *speaker* if they meant to deceive.
- **Fiction and art** marked as fiction → **safe.** An actor delivering lines, a novelist, a child narrating a game — no pain. The book interrogates this exemption: *why is the stage exempt and the witness stand not?* (A recurring, unresolved unease — art as sanctioned lying.)
- **Promises (statements about the future):** A promise made with sincere intent to keep it → safe, even if later broken. A promise made with no intent to keep it → present-tense lie about one's own intent → it hurts.
- **Opinions / matters of taste** ("this is the best restaurant in town") → safe unless asserted as fact the speaker privately rejects.
- **Bullshit vs. lying (canonical distinction):** The careless person who doesn't care whether a claim is true (the bullshitter) is only hurt to the degree they *suspect* it's false. Pure indifference partially shields — another reason the shallow thrive and the conscientious bleed.
- **Self-deception is unstable:** belief built to dodge pain can crack under direct, specific confrontation. Crane's serenity is real but not infinitely robust; the climax tests exactly this seam.
- **The condition does not compel disclosure, accuracy of memory, or goodness.** You can be cruel and true. You can be wrong and painless. You can withhold. It enforces only this: *do not assert what you know to be false.*

## What the condition is NOT (guardrails against drift)
- Not telepathy. Daniel cannot read minds — only observe who suffers and who doesn't.
- Not a truth-extractor. It cannot make anyone speak. It only makes speech costly.
- Not selective or aimable. No "targeting." Always-on, room-radius, everyone.
- Not curable, transferable, or (as far as anyone proves) explicable. Theories abound; **none is confirmed.**
- Not magic with exceptions of convenience. **If a scene needs the rule bent, the scene is wrong.** Rigor is the source of the book's power.
