## Daniel Mercer — protagonist
- **Age:** 44. Lean, well-dressed, prematurely grey at the temples; a face built for sincerity he never felt. A voice people trust on instinct — his instrument and his sin.
- **Profession (pre-onset):** Partner at **Vance Mercer Albright**, an elite crisis-communications and reputation-management firm. The man corporations and senators call at 2 a.m. He doesn't lie crudely; he *reframes.* He once made a chemical spill into "a community resilience story." His gift is making the false feel true — to others and, crucially, to himself.
- **The defining sin:** Months before onset, Daniel orchestrated the burial of a true story: a whistleblower, **Priya Raman** (an engineer at a client firm, **Halcombe Aerospace**), reported a falsified safety test. Daniel destroyed her credibility — leaked her therapy records, seeded the narrative that she was unstable and vengeful. The story died. Priya lost everything; later, a part failed (a regional jet, eleven dead). Daniel told himself the failure was unrelated. **He still tells himself that.** (This is the lie he most needs to be true — and the belief loophole means it doesn't hurt him *because he believes it.* Until it cracks.)
- **Onset:** The morning after the firm's biggest "win" — a virtuoso lie that saves a client and ruins a man. The book never confirms cause and effect.
- **Voice/interiority:** Dry, precise, observant, armored with irony. A connoisseur of how language manages people. Post-onset, his interior monologue (where he can still lie to himself) becomes the last place he's free — and the book quietly weaponizes that against the reader.
- **Arc:** Cynic who believes nothing → forced into a sincerity he despises → discovers that being unable to lie has not made him good → must choose to tell the one truth that will cost him everything (his complicity in Priya's destruction), the truth no curse can compel out of him because he half-believes the lie. **He is not redeemed by the power. He redeems himself in spite of it.**
- **Wants:** To get his old life back / to make the condition stop. **Needs:** To stop being a man who is right and start being a man who is honest with himself.
- **Epithet:** The press dubs him **"the Believer"** (Tamsin's headline, ironically; he believes nothing). The name sticks and metastasizes into a movement he never sought.

## Nadia Mercer — wife
- **Age:** 41. A pediatric oncologist — a woman whose whole professional life is the careful, merciful management of unbearable truths told to parents of dying children. *She lies kindly for a living, the way Daniel lied cruelly.* The two of them are the same crime in different keys.
- **The secret:** She has been, for a year, in a tender, mostly-unconsummated relationship of the heart with a colleague — not yet an affair in the body, fully one in the wanting. On the morning of onset, in their kitchen, Daniel asks an ordinary question and the field makes her answer it. **The marriage's reckoning is the first casualty of the field, before Daniel even understands what he is.**
- **Function:** The intimate stakes. She forces the question the book keeps asking: *is a marriage survivable without the small mercies of untruth?* Her professional ethic (the merciful lie) is the humane counter-argument to Daniel's new absolutism.
- **Arc:** From wronged-and-wronging wife → to the person who articulates the book's deepest objection to the condition → to an ambiguous, hard-won, clear-eyed separateness. Not a tidy reconciliation; something truer and sadder.

## June Mercer — daughter
- **Age:** 9. Sharp, funny, anxious. The child in a house where no one can lie.
- **Function:** The cost of the condition rendered unbearable and small. No "everything's going to be fine." No Santa (a quietly devastating Christmas chapter). She learns to ask her father questions like a prosecutor because she's the only person who can get true answers in a world of adults who can't. She also learns the loophole young and frighteningly fast: *if you believe it, it doesn't hurt.* June believing her own comforts is one of the book's most haunting images.
- **Arc:** Innocence → precocious, slightly broken competence → a final scene that holds the whole book's ambiguity in a child's sentence.

## Tamsin Okafor — journalist
- **Age:** 37. Investigative reporter at a serious-but-struggling outlet (*The Lede*). Brilliant, ambitious, ethically real but not saintly. British-Nigerian, raised in the city.
- **Function:** Makes Daniel public; coins "the Believer"; becomes the closest thing he has to a true friend and also the person most willing to use him. Embodies the book's question about journalism: *truth as vocation vs. truth as content.*
- **Key beat:** She brings Daniel to Marcus Reyes (the exoneration) — the high point of the condition's good. She also, later, puts him in a room she shouldn't, for a story, with a cost.
- **Arc:** Believer in truth-as-light → witness to truth-as-weapon → chastened, complicit, ultimately the one who helps Daniel tell *his own* truth rather than everyone else's.

## Elias Crane — antagonist
- **Age:** 52. Former tent-revival preacher from the heartland; charismatic, warm, genuinely kind-seeming, with the unkillable calm of a man who has resolved every doubt. Rising populist figure — first a media phenomenon, then a candidate.
- **The terror of him:** He stands in the field and feels **nothing.** Not because he's honest — because he *believes,* totally, including the things that aren't true. He has perfected sincerity. He experiences his own propaganda as revelation. **He is the loophole made flesh.**
- **His doctrine — "the Conviction":** He reframes Daniel's condition as a divine sieve separating the faithful (who feel no pain because they believe) from the doubting wicked (who suffer). *"The Believer burns the liars and blesses the faithful. Pain is just doubt leaving the body. Believe, and you will never hurt again."* He turns a truth-enforcer into an engine of mass self-deception. Under his teaching, **the cure for the curse is to stop knowing what's true.**
- **Why he's unbeatable by the power:** You cannot expose a man who is not lying. Crane's falsehoods are sincere. Daniel's superpower is *useless* against him — the climax must be fought on other ground.
- **Crucially not a cartoon:** Crane offers people something real — relief, belonging, an end to the agony of doubt in a world suddenly full of unbearable truths. He is the answer to a real pain the condition causes. That's what makes him dangerous and the book honest.
- **Arc:** Fringe preacher → prophet of the Conviction → political force → final public confrontation with Daniel where sincerity meets the one thing it can't metabolize.

## Marcus Reyes — the exoneree (moral counterweight)
- **Age:** 34. Eleven years into a life sentence for a murder he didn't commit, convicted on a lying witness and a coerced statement.
- **Function:** The condition's purest good. In a re-examination held in Daniel's presence (Part II/III), the recanting witness *cannot sustain the lie* and Daniel's silent suffering-as-evidence breaks the case open. Marcus walks free. **The book needs this to be unambiguously good — and then needs to show that being freed is not the same as being whole.** Marcus out in a world that owes him everything and gives him nothing is the other half of the truth.
- **Arc:** Wrongful prisoner → exonerated man → the friend who, late in the book, tells Daniel the plainest truth of all about what the condition is worth.

## Priya Raman — the ghost / the buried truth
- The whistleblower Daniel destroyed. Appears in memory, in a redacted dossier, and — decisively — **alive,** in the final movement. Daniel's lie about her is the lie he must finally break inside himself. She is the human face of the eleven dead and of everything Daniel "reframed."

## Supporting / institutional
- **Gideon Vance** — Daniel's senior partner and mentor; the firm's conscience-free patriarch; teaches that "the truth is just the story nobody's bothered to beat yet." A foil; the world Daniel came from.
- **Renata Holm** — a federal official (Department of Justice / intelligence liaison) who recognizes the field as a national-security asset and brings Daniel into the **black-site arc** (Part IV). Not a sadist — a patriot, which is worse. Argues that a man who can make any prisoner unable to lie is too valuable to leave free.
- **Victor Salt** — tech magnate (firm: **Halcyon**) who wants to study, replicate, monetize, or contain the effect. Comic-sinister; the satire of techno-solutionism. Funds research into "synthetic conviction." Mostly Part III.
- **Father Anton** — an old Jesuit, late-book, who offers the one religious reading of the condition that *isn't* Crane's: that a curse forcing truth without granting mercy is the opposite of grace. The book's quiet theological counterweight.
- **The chorus** — voices that appear only in the dossier interludes: ad executives, talk-radio hosts, message-board posters, a couples therapist, a poker pro, a senator's aide, a stand-up comedian who builds an act on the fact that jokes don't hurt. The satire lives here.
