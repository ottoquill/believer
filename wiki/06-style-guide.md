# 06 — Style Guide (read before drafting any chapter)

This guide exists so chapters drafted separately read as **one book by one author.** Obey it literally.

## POV & tense
- **Daniel's chapters:** Third-person **limited**, **past tense**, close on Daniel. We get his interiority — and remember: *his interior is the one place he can still lie,* so his self-justifications about Priya read as plausible until they don't. Never head-hop within a scene.
- **Dossier interludes:** Various forms (see below), each in its native register. These are the satirical/panoramic counterpoint.
- No second person. No present tense except inside quoted documents that naturally use it (ad copy, transcripts).

## Voice & prose values (the target)
- **Register:** Literary, controlled, exact. Think of the love-child of **José Saramago's premise-rigor, Kazuo Ishiguro's restraint, and the satirical bite of George Saunders / Joseph Heller** — with the propulsion of a good thriller underneath.
- **Sentences:** Vary length deliberately. Long, clause-stacked observation followed by a short, flat landing. Earn every aphorism; cut three out of four.
- **Diction:** Precise, never ornate for its own sake. Daniel notices *how language manages people* — that's his expertise and our lens. Comms/PR vocabulary used with insider accuracy ("reframe," "get ahead of," "the narrative," "optics," "managed disclosure").
- **Wit:** Dry, never zany in Daniel's chapters; the zaniness lives in the dossier. Irony is a scalpel, not a clown nose.
- **Show the rule through bodies and behavior,** not exposition. We learn the physics by watching people wince, drift to the room's edge, step into hallways to take calls.
- **No info-dumps.** Never explain the condition in narration beyond what a character could plausibly observe. The wiki knows the rules; the *prose* discovers them.
- **Sentiment discipline:** The book has enormous feeling but earns it by under-playing. When a scene is devastating, write it cooler. Let the reader supply the tears.

## Hard craft rules
- **Said is invisible; prefer it.** Avoid "he opined / she expostulated." Action beats over adverbs.
- **No clichés.** Especially no truth/lie clichés ("the truth will set you free," "tangled web," "moment of truth") *except* in the mouths of characters who would actually use them (Crane, ad copy) — there, irony.
- **Specificity over abstraction.** Not "he felt pain," but the exact, escalating sensory signature (foil, battery, the flashbulb, the nosebleed). Calibrate pain to lie-size every single time — this is the book's physics made felt.
- **Dialogue does double work:** in the field, *what people don't say* and *where they pause* carries the scene. Master the eloquent silence and the legible omission.
- **Chapter openings:** drop into scene or image, not throat-clearing. **Chapter endings:** land on a turn, an image, or a line that resonates — never trail off.
- **Earn the aphorism, then stop.** One quotable line per chapter, maximum. The book will be very quotable if we ration it.

## Continuity discipline
- Match facts in [02-characters.md](02-characters.md), [03-timeline.md](03-timeline.md), [07-canon-and-glossary.md](07-canon-and-glossary.md).
- Pain calibration must be **consistent**: same lie-magnitude → same pain-family. Don't let a white lie cause a nosebleed.
- The field's range/limits never change. If a scene seems to need the rule bent, the scene is wrong.
- Track who knows what about Daniel and when (pre-public vs. post-"Believer" world). See timeline.

## Dossier interlude formats (rotate; ~8–11 total across the book, short: 300–1200 words)
Each is a found document. Examples to draw from:
- A **news article** from *The Lede* or a tabloid (the coining of "the Believer").
- **Ad copy / a brand brief** for a product that exploits the field ("Honesty-Free Zones™," noise-cancelling "privacy booths," a dating app that pairs you *outside* the field).
- A **sermon transcript** from Crane (the Conviction doctrine, in his own seductive voice).
- A **couples-therapy session transcript** in a world without white lies.
- A **redacted federal memo** on the field as an asset (the black-site arc, bureaucratic horror).
- A **message-board / comment thread** (the loophole as folk wisdom: "just believe it lol").
- A **stand-up comedy set** built on the fact that jokes don't hurt (art's exemption, weaponized for laughs and unease).
- A **product recall / aviation report fragment** (Halcombe; eleven dead; the buried truth surfacing in cold officialese).
- A **VMA internal "narrative strategy" deck** from before onset (Daniel's old trade, satirized).
- A **personal ad / obituary / school newsletter** showing the condition's reach into ordinary life.
Keep them sharp, satirical, and *load-bearing* — each must advance theme or plot, not just decorate.

## Content & rating
- **Adult literary fiction.** Profanity allowed, used precisely (not constant). Sexual content: present but not explicit; tasteful, emotionally driven. Violence: the body-horror of large lies (blood, seizure) and the institutional violence of the black site — rendered with restraint, never gratuitous. One off-page suicide (handled with care, not detail; thematically essential, not exploitative).
- Aim: a book a serious literary-prize jury and a wide Kindle readership can both finish. Provocative, humane, not edgy-for-its-own-sake.

## Length targets
- Daniel chapters: **2,600–3,400 words.**
- Dossier interludes: **300–1,200 words.**
- Whole book target: **~85,000–92,000 words.**
- Chapters are numbered continuously (1..N) across parts; parts get a title page.
